The Pantry Project

A local mission built into The Happy Pantry

The Pantry Project is a mission of The Happy Pantry focused on helping local food banks across New Hampshire and Vermont. Each month, we choose a specific community and run a fundraising campaign to purchase food and supplies directly for a local pantry. Community support helps keep The Happy Pantry free to use while allowing us to provide simple meal planning tools, recipes, and up-to-date nutrition information that is easy to find, practical, and accessible for everyone.


The Pantry Project is operated through The Happy Pantry LLC by a small group of people passionate about improving access to food and nutrition. Keeping the project simple allows more of the funds raised to go toward food purchases and supplies for local pantries without the added administrative cost and complexity of maintaining a formal nonprofit organization. Contributions are voluntary and are not tax-deductible.

100% of donations support The Pantry Project. About 90% of funds raised go toward food purchases and supplies for local food banks, while the remainder helps cover administrative costs and the transportation and coordination needed to purchase and deliver food through the project.

Simple by design This project is meant to feel direct, local, and easy to understand — free tools for everyday use, plus visible support for nearby communities.

How the Pantry Project works

The model is intentionally simple: one campaign, one community, and one clear opportunity at a time to help support local food access.

1. Choose a town

Each month, we focus on a specific community in New Hampshire or Vermont and identify a local pantry to support.

2. Run a campaign

We share a simple fundraising goal through the website, email, and social channels so people can support the effort.

3. Purchase food

Funds are used to purchase food and supplies intended to support the selected pantry and its surrounding community.

4. Share updates

As the project grows, we will post campaign updates, impact summaries, and optional photos to keep support visible and real.

Why food pantries matter

Food stress does not always look dramatic from the outside. Sometimes it looks like skipped meals, repetitive low-cost groceries, or families trying to stretch what they have for just one more week. The Pantry Project exists to support local food access in a way that feels direct, practical, and community-centered.

  • Local focus across New Hampshire and Vermont
  • Monthly campaigns with a specific community goal
  • Free recipes, planning tools, and easy-to-find nutrition information
  • A small, direct model built for clarity and trust
