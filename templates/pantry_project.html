The Pantry Project

Helping local pantries fill the gaps that food donations alone do not solve.

The Pantry Project is a mission of The Happy Pantry focused on helping local food pantries and community food efforts across New Hampshire and Vermont. Support can help fund food, supplies, equipment, transportation, and practical improvements that make it easier for communities to access nourishment with dignity and consistency.

Support the Project How It Works
More than food delivery

Sometimes the biggest need is food. Other times it is shelving, storage, refrigeration support, pantry supplies, transportation, or practical upgrades that help a pantry serve more people more effectively. The goal is simple: help communities close the gaps that stand in the way of access.

Filling the gaps

Local support should solve real pantry needs

The Pantry Project is not only about bringing food to food banks in New Hampshire and Vermont. It is about identifying the practical needs that help food pantries fulfill their mission more effectively. That can mean helping with food purchases, but it can also mean helping fund equipment, supplies, shelving, storage tools, transportation, or other small improvements that help food move more efficiently to the people who need it.


Every pantry and every community has different gaps. In some places the need may be staples or fresh protein. In others, it may be the equipment or organization needed to safely store, sort, and distribute food at a better scale. The Pantry Project is designed to stay flexible enough to support what actually matters most.

What support can really do Support can help strengthen the whole process — from purchasing food to improving the space, tools, and logistics that help pantries serve their communities better.

What support can help fund

The Pantry Project is built to support the practical side of community food access — not just what gets donated, but what helps those donations work better.

Food and pantry staples

Fresh produce, protein, shelf-stable items, and practical food purchases that help pantries respond to current community needs.

Supplies and storage tools

Bins, shelving, organizational materials, and pantry supplies that improve how food is sorted, stored, and distributed.

Equipment and improvements

Refrigeration support, basic equipment, and other practical upgrades that improve fulfillment and strengthen pantry operations.

Transportation and coordination

The planning, travel, pickup, and delivery support needed to purchase and move food or supplies through the project.

Local pantry support

Flexible support for the practical needs of individual communities, so efforts can match the realities of each pantry’s situation.

Community access

The real goal is not just moving items, but improving how well local pantries can reach and serve people in their community.

How the Pantry Project works

The process is meant to stay simple, visible, and focused on practical results at the local level.

1. Choose a community

We focus on local food access efforts across New Hampshire and Vermont, one community at a time.

2. Identify the gap

We look at the practical need — whether that is food, supplies, equipment, transportation, or a small operational improvement.

3. Put support to work

Funds are directed toward the items or support needed to help that pantry or community food effort function better.

4. Share the impact

As the project grows, we will continue sharing updates and examples of where support is going and why it matters.

How support is allocated

100% of donations support The Pantry Project. About 90% of funds raised go toward food purchases and supplies for local food banks, while the remainder helps cover administrative costs and the transportation and coordination needed to purchase and deliver food through the project.

Why this mission matters

Community food support is strongest when it is practical, local, and focused on the real needs that affect distribution and access.

It keeps help flexible

Every pantry is different. A flexible model lets support go where it can actually improve fulfillment, not just where assumptions say it should.

It improves local capacity

Better tools, better storage, and better logistics can strengthen a pantry’s ability to serve people well beyond a single delivery.

It keeps community first

The goal is not to overcomplicate support. It is to direct practical resources where they can create meaningful, local impact.

About The Pantry Project

The Pantry Project is a mission of The Happy Pantry focused on helping local food banks across New Hampshire and Vermont. Each month, we choose a specific community and direct support toward food, supplies, or other practical pantry needs that help improve local access. Community support helps keep The Happy Pantry free to use while allowing us to provide simple meal planning tools, recipes, and up-to-date nutrition information that is easy to find, practical, and accessible for everyone.

How it is operated

The Pantry Project is operated through The Happy Pantry LLC by a small group of people passionate about improving access to food and nutrition. Keeping the project simple allows more of the funds raised to go toward food purchases, supplies, and practical pantry support without the added administrative cost and complexity of maintaining a formal nonprofit organization. Contributions are voluntary and are not tax-deductible.
